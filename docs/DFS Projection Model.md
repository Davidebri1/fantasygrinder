You'll also notice a neat trick to generate projections. it's the same trick that everyone is using, unbeknownst to them or not. 

linear is logic. y = mx+b is always m as slope. unless you dont know the logic then m becomes a variable. so you get x^2. which you know is complex, imaginary, not solvable and thus...not real. no projections are ever formed off of an unknown or subjective datum. because of this. likely instinctually, but this is the science of it. without a datum, you will always end up with complex formula. and complex formulas cant be sold because they expose themselves absolutely.

what does that mean? it means the absence of logic, the lack of sight of it, or the twisting of it, makes this lack recognizable instantly. it cant be hidden, except by feigning technical constraints or some other form of lack of control. deflection. this isnt a good position to put ypurself in. so they dont even consider it. therefore, every projection is a linear model. that means two things that you know are juxtaposed against one another to form the logical basis. points scored per game. easy. and...price. easy. one cannot go up while the other goes down. and vice versa. so you cant use that, since it doesnt let you validate your slope. it's in concert. so you need to get the exact opposite. you form logic by demonstrating contrast. so you take their highest scoring games, and their lowest scoring game as your too opposites. since you cant be high scoring and low scoring in one game. this cut off can be decided any way you like.

you project the highest scoring by forecasting the average of the highest scoring games, regardless of opponent. and lowest scoring games, regardless of opponent. use all the games. so your splitting your data into two halves. high and low, ad getting two means. you then test that average against the next game and athe next game, adding that game to your projection average only when they are classified as belonging to the high or low. if his actual qualifies for low, you put it there. you then test the data of each sample size for normality. If normal, you test them against one another for sameness; they shouldnt be the same. if they are, you project using the raw average of he two samples. unlikely but true.  

if they are non normal within one half or another, you separate out the multimodal data. the peaks that dont belong. sort it out. then you reevaluate until you get normal curve. not the average, and median, and mode. IQR can be used later if necessary. With the multimodal anomalies removed, you then investigate that data for a common thread. this belongs to a different population, statistically, so there is a factor. this ranges. but is objective, not speculative, in its discovery, because it willl be common amongst all your data. If it isn't, separate the ones that dont have that in common. 

by ding this, you discover your slope. M. it could be the month of december. it could be playoffs. it could be first time starting. it could be number of pass attempts. it could be minutes played. these last 2 are typically assumed i imagine, and not necessarily empirically derived. by empirically deriving it, you maintain 100% confidence and control. because you are continuusly doing this evaluation game to game, you can always update to the latest trends. because they will show up. and it's not a guessng game. a rolling trend or associated tendency. it's always going to be based on the observation of fact. this factor is common to all of these outputs. it can be multiple factors common to all of them. you simply pick one of them (not multiple) and test that. 

once you have the factor, you apply it as you would any slope. by its coefficient based on frequency or likelihood. usually this will be 100%. so if a player is low scoring when his fav receiver is not playing, that % of the receivr not playing gets multiplied by the average of low scoring games. then you do the same for the high scoring side. and you take the probability of that factor. lets say home games are his outlier high scoring games. if it's a home game, you multiple his home scoring outlier average by 1(100% chance of home game), and use that number to add to the low scoring number.

this highlights the strength of your slopes, while respecting natural variability and unknowns. you get access to boom or bust games, upsets, etc. that you wouldnt if you just took rollling seasonal averages. by using the average category only when youre uncertain (no known outlier slope),  you directly attach the probability to that outcome to that outcome, using the even split between good and bad as the floor. you would think this limits results, but no it doesnt.  bad players only proect to break out when it's certain. and their breakout is limited by their other breakouts. at worst, they will have an above average game in these favorable situations. if you guess the projection, or dont derive it in some certain way, you will always miss the floor, because the floor isnt accounted for. the floor should be natural variablity, which is why i always respect the even split, as it represents normality, reality. half high  half low = total projection. the skewness  happens on the side, and grows  in power in relation to uncertainty, lack of data. as it should. so youre not guessing on a random number, or quadratically deriving probabilities stacked on one another. you're simply using actual observable fact t create binary, not continous, outcomes.  it ealistically accomodates every scenario without making any guesses or assumptions. all conditionals are logical conditionals. a 50% chance to play or not play. a 25% chance of rain. a 100% chance of a road game. a 100% chance of facing a top 10 ranked pass  rush. you see how th subjective things are being resolved to objective observation? the probability was external, and i turn it into a cosntant, a given. by doing that, i dont inherit a variable, an unknown. i inheret a resolved constant.
